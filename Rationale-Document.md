# Leadership Portfolio Redesign

### Product Management Assignment — DeepThought / PDGMS

---

# 1. Design Challenges Addressed

## Challenge 1 — The Signal

### My Position

The first few seconds could only reflect back strongly if it can be expressed in a visual medium.

### My Solution

I have chosen to use colors and doodles or icons that can reflect back on the journey within an easy medium of expression. So my solution was using it through colors. That is why the color palette that I chose is mostly green and white, nothing dark, black, or blue which could directly reflect back as a very hard or serious kind of website to look at. Rather, white and green make it feel calmer and lighter.

In fact, the decision of not using red in the whole website makes it feel more optimistic and lighter emotionally. That itself helps the experience reflect more like an actual pitch deck rather than a monitoring dashboard.

---

## Challenge 2 — Story vs Data Ordering

### My Position

Story versus data ordering is slightly tricky, given the fact that there are different ways of viewing the same thing. If I am an employee myself, I would like to first look at positive things before looking at numbers which could brutally hit me with reality. But if I am a supervisor or a person of authority, I would rather prefer to immediately look at the raw numbers.

So for that reason, my standing was pretty simple. As a developer, I wanted to choose a very mild data-first and then story approach.

### My Solution

My solution to this problem was simple. Put in a visual medium of data first that does not exactly look like a dashboard, which could otherwise reflect very serious and raw numbers, but rather choose a milder and more human way of presenting the same information.

The idea was to make it feel more like a journey that has been completed and what is left to achieve next. That way of representation makes it slightly easier on the eye for the employee while still preserving clarity for supervisors.

That is why I chose a milder visual approach instead of traditional pie charts, aggressive bar graphs, or raw KPI-heavy layouts.

---

## Challenge 4 — Constraints Without Anxiety

### My Position

At the very core of it, we still have to deal with real numbers and real problems which have to be addressed. But while doing that, it is also important to keep the experience optimistic for the employee.

We should rather choose to highlight areas where the employee has grown more strongly instead of emotionally centering the experience around where they lacked.

### My Solution

For that reason, the solution for me was again simple — choosing the fields where the employee had made greater progress, putting them first, and showcasing them using greener, brighter, optimistic colors.

Even the constraints themselves or areas where the employee is blocked can be addressed in a slightly different way simply by shifting the perspective. Instead of showing “what blocked me,” the interface reframes them more like “what is left to achieve next.”

That is also why I chose to rename that section closer to “targets” rather than making it feel like a failure-reporting section.

Again, visually avoiding red and instead using orange made the experience much easier and lighter on the eye.

---

## Challenge 5 — Portability and Identity

### My Position

The portfolio should feel personal because at the end of the day it represents the employee himself and not just company data. If the employee cannot emotionally connect with the portfolio, then it again just becomes another dashboard or reporting system.

The identity therefore should feel visible enough for the employee to feel ownership over it.

### My Solution

For that reason, I chose to make identity slightly more visible throughout the interface itself through the name, progression status, and the overall narrative-first structure.

Even the UID and progression timeline were kept visible because they reflect continuity and long-term growth rather than just monthly reporting.

The whole idea was to make it feel closer to a personal career pitch deck rather than a company analytics page.

---

# 2. Assumptions About User Behavior

The core assumption behind the redesign was that the employee is not opening the portfolio just to look at a report or a dashboard, but rather to understand what they have achieved so far and where they are progressing next.

Hence, the main assumption was that the primary viewer is the employee himself and not only a supervisor.

The second assumption was that the raw numbers and metrics are secondary. The more important aspect of the entire experience was motivating the employee and helping them emotionally connect with their own growth journey.

The third assumption was regarding the data itself. I assumed that the employee would generally show progressive growth over time, and therefore the portfolio should visually support continuity, optimism, and movement forward.

I also assumed that employees emotionally connect more strongly with completed work and visible progress than with aggressive KPI systems or monitoring-style reporting.

---

# 3. Tradeoffs and Limitations

One major tradeoff while designing this was balancing storytelling with structured backend data. Since the backend already had fixed metrics and attributes associated with it, the challenge was presenting those same metrics in a way that did not immediately feel like a traditional monitoring dashboard.

Another tradeoff was reducing visual clutter without losing measurable clarity. If too much data was shown directly, the interface again started feeling stressful and metric-heavy. But reducing too much data also risked making the portfolio feel less accountable or informative.

I also intentionally avoided heavy chart systems, pie charts, aggressive KPI layouts, and red warning indicators because they immediately shifted the emotional tone toward employee monitoring instead of career reflection.

The overall challenge throughout the redesign was balancing optimism with honesty. The experience still needed to represent real performance and real blockers while avoiding the feeling of operational pressure.

---

# 4. What I Would Improve With More Time

With more time, I would explore more adaptive storytelling systems where the portfolio itself changes slightly depending on the employee’s monthly progress and emotional state.

I would also improve the career trajectory section more deeply with interactive timelines and progression-based reflections across multiple months rather than only a single monthly snapshot.

Another area I would improve is creating slightly different viewing modes for employees and supervisors because both consume the same information differently. Employees emotionally connect more with progress and storytelling, while supervisors may prefer quicker access to structured metrics and measurable outcomes.

I would also experiment more with motion, transitions, and subtle visual feedback systems to make the overall experience feel more alive while still preserving the calm and minimal direction of the interface.

---

# 5. Conclusion

The overall intention behind the redesign was to shift the Leadership Portfolio from a stressful operational dashboard into something that feels more reflective, optimistic, and personal for the employee himself.

While preserving the backend structure and measurable data, the main focus was to make the experience emotionally lighter and more journey-oriented rather than purely analytical.

Instead of presenting the employee with a cold system of reporting, the intention was to create a space that reflects growth, continuity, progress, and ownership over one’s own journey.
